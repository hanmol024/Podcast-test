Here are the biggest news stories and how they connect. Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive after President Trump personally pressed it, eleven days after he resigned over affairs, one with an executive tied to a suspected Chinese intelligence asset. The Nasdaq rose six point five percent and Nvidia added nearly a trillion dollars of value; Korea's Kospi jumped seven percent. Yet lawmakers of both parties are demanding he testify, he is due before senators on the third of December, and the same day Washington blacklisted Megaspeed, the firm at the centre of the chip-smuggling case. Markets are pricing the AI trade as too big to be allowed to fail. My reading: that is political insurance, not a changed fundamental, and it lasts only as long as Congress lets it.

Politics.

United States. Democrats have won the House by the largest margin in fifty years, flipping forty-seven seats with a possible forty-nine. California's Jimmy Gomez resigns after an ethics report found unwelcome sexual advances.

UK. Prime Minister Burnham says rejoining the EU without a second referendum would be unthinkable, his strongest pledge yet to consult voters. Officials insist he is not backtracking on closer ties after the Brussels summit.

Europe. Spain's election campaign is reopening civil war wounds as the far right nears government; a new poll says nearly a third of voters mainly want Sánchez gone. Estonia is closing its border with Russia, which has protested, and Finland summoned Russia's ambassador over a suspected helicopter incursion.

Mexico. Friday's capture in Sinaloa of the cartel leader known as Chapo Isidro, Fausto Meza Flores, is now a sovereignty test. Sheinbaum says US intelligence helped; Mexico says Americans were not on the ground. Mexico will try him itself despite a US extradition request. Conflict and world affairs.

Gaza. Israeli strikes killed at least three people at a police post in Khan Younis, and Reuters reports a United Nations relief agency worker delivering water was among the dead. Winter is arriving with shelter aid short, United Nations agencies warn. Oxfam says more than ninety-nine percent of Gaza shows environmental contamination.

West Bank. Settler raids on Palestinian villages drew rare condemnation from Israeli leaders, including President Herzog, as Jewish terrorism; France condemned them too. A rights group says attacks on Palestinian medics are up three hundred fifty percent. US envoy Jared Kushner told New York Magazine Israel is on a path to becoming a global-pariah state.

Iran. The US naval blockade stands; Iran says three ships caught fire after hitting mines in Hormuz and vows to use every tool. Washington sanctioned dozens in Iran's shadow banking network; Qatar is trying to revive talks.

Ukraine. Kyiv confirmed sea drones sank the Russian corvette Merkury at Benghazi in Libya, prompting a United Nations call for an inquiry. Reports say Russia may put a nuclear weapon in orbit; NATO vows space defence.

Elsewhere. Tajikistan is leaving the International Criminal Court, the fourth country to do so since a US push. India pledged one point six billion dollars to flood-hit Nepal. Disasters: a factory fire in Indonesia killed twenty-four with about thirty missing.

Economy and markets.

Equities. The Huang rally has two readings. One says it removes a governance discount on the firm anchoring AI capital spending. The other says a leveraged AI cycle just proved it needs political rescue, which raises the risk premium, not lowers it. Either way, my inference is that this week's cheaper equity currency feeds the stock-funded deal wave below.

Deals as signal. CrowdStrike will buy Zscaler for forty-three point nine billion dollars in an all-stock deal, Amazon is reported to pay seven point five billion for Decart, and Vår Energi is buying ConocoPhillips's Norwegian assets for four point four billion. Big, stock-paid deals say boards are confident of equity valuations, not cheap debt; CrowdStrike fell twelve percent on the news, so investors are not.

Oil. Crude sits near a three-month low as supply rises, despite the Hormuz mines. Markets are betting damage stays contained.

Mexico and Brazil. Mexico's economy grew three point three percent in the third quarter, its best since twenty twenty-three, with optimism about a US deal. Brazil cut its twenty twenty-seven growth forecast to one point eight percent and raised inflation to four point five, blaming high rates.
